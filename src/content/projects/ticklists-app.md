  <h3>Product Walkthrough</h3><p>Most list apps delete or move an item the moment it's ticked, so the whole list has to be rebuilt from scratch next time. Ticklists keeps every item on the list instead (until it's deleted). On a shopping list, you tick an item as it runs out and would be needed in the next shopping trip, then filter to "Ticked only" whilst in the shop, and untick as things are bought, or tap "Untick all" once everything's unpacked at home. On a routine, it works the other way round: tick a task off as you complete it, then reset the list for the next day. Either way, nothing gets retyped.</p><h4>Signing up</h4><p>Free and Pro limits are shown before signup, not hidden behind a paywall.</p><div class="tl-block"><div class="tl-row"><img alt="Ticklists login screen with Register and Login tabs, and a Pro upgrade offer" src="/images/projects/ticklists-app/login-top.png" loading="lazy"><img alt="Register screen scrolled to show the Free and Pro plan comparison" src="/images/projects/ticklists-app/register-bottom.png" loading="lazy"></div></div><h4>Shopping lists that sort themselves</h4><p>A Master List gets bulk-pasted in once, with sections if wanted, then shared with named household members by email. projectInDetail: |-
  <h3>Background</h3><p>Every list app I tried either made me retype the same items constantly, or had no way to group items by section at all. Google Sheets was the closest fit, because it let my household see live ticks together, but it had no search and no sections. Task apps like Asana solved the structure problem but were built for work teams, not a weekly food shop.</p><h3>Competitive landscape</h3><p>Nothing on the market lets a household bulk-upload a master list and have it reorder itself to match a specific store's layout. Apps like AnyList and Listonic sort by generic aisle category, but not per store, and both lose items the moment they're ticked, forcing a retype. Bring! solves live sharing well but has no master list and no store-specific ordering either.</p><h3>Who it's for</h3><ul><li><strong>People with ADHD</strong> — a large, vocal audience whose main complaint about shopping is forgetting items; a master list that never disappears solves that directly.</li><li><strong>Multi-person households</strong> — everyone ticks off what's run out, live, without retyping or re-sharing.</li><li><strong>People running repetitive routines or property maintenance</strong> — daily or weekly tasks that need to survive from one day to the next without being rebuilt.</li><li><strong>Small businesses with recurring checklists</strong> — shared operational lists, such as opening and closing routines or equipment maintenance, that stay the same week to week.</li></ul>
